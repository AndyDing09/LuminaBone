\title{Photometric-Stereo Surface Reconstruction and CT Registration for a
Near-Coaxial Endoscopic Rig: A Complete Methods and Implementation Report}

\author{A.~Ding \textit{et al.}%
\thanks{Manuscript draft. Correspondence: andyding09@gmail.com.}}

Photometric-Stereo Surface Reconstruction and CT Registration}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This report documents, in full, a pipeline that reconstructs the surface of
exposed bone from a monocular endoscope carrying four near-coaxial LEDs, and
registers the reconstruction to a preoperative CT for quantitative validation.
We give the complete image-formation model, the linearised far-field and
near-field photometric-stereo solves, the Frankot--Chellappa normal integration,
an inverse-square brightness-to-range baseline, camera calibration, the
Perspective-$n$-Point (PnP) pose and affine depth calibration used for CT
registration, the surface-extraction and meshing stages, and the evaluation
metrics (three-dimensional fiducial registration error, plane-fit deviation,
working distance, and marker coplanarity). All equations are stated explicitly so
the pipeline is reproducible from this document and the released code. On an
instrumented lumbar specimen, the near-field model registers three vertebral
levels to \SIrange{1.3}{1.7}{\milli\meter} 3-D fiducial registration error and
removes the systematic low-frequency ``bowl'' produced by the far-field
approximation; the inverse-square baseline and two failure modes are reported and
analysed without omission.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Photometric stereo, near-field illumination, endoscopy, surface reconstruction,
CT registration, Perspective-$n$-Point, fiducial registration error.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction and Scope}
\IEEEPARstart{T}{his} report is a self-contained specification of the LuminaBone
reconstruction and registration pipeline. It is deliberately exhaustive at the
level of equations and algorithms: every stage from raw pixels to a CT-registered
triangle mesh is written down so that the results can be reproduced independently.
Positioning, clinical framing, and novelty are treated elsewhere; here we document
\emph{what is computed and how}.

The pipeline has three parts. \textbf{(i)~Photometric-stereo depth}
(Sec.~\ref{sec:ps}) recovers a relative surface from four single-illuminant
images. \textbf{(ii)~CT registration} (Sec.~\ref{sec:reg}) anchors that surface to
absolute CT coordinates through dual-modality fiducials, a PnP pose, and an affine
depth calibration. \textbf{(iii)~Surface extraction and evaluation}
(Secs.~\ref{sec:surf}--\ref{sec:results}) mask, mesh, and score the result against
CT ground truth. Notation is summarised in Table~\ref{tab:notation}.

\section{Imaging System and Data Acquisition}
The endoscope has a native resolution of $640\times480$ and carries four LEDs on a
ring of radius $a_r=\SI{6.05}{\milli\meter}$ about the optical axis, at azimuths
\ang{0}, \ang{90}, \ang{180}, \ang{270}. The measured field width is
\SIrange{37}{43}{\milli\meter}; at a working distance $D$ each LED subtends a
half-angle
\begin{equation}
\alpha=\arctan\!\big(a_r/D\big),
\label{eq:alpha}
\end{equation}
so at $D=\SI{55}{\milli\meter}$, $\alpha\approx\ang{6.3}$ and the rig is
\emph{near-coaxial}: the tilt-encoding lateral component of each light vector is
only $\sin\alpha\approx0.11$ of unit length. Retroreflective fiducial markers are
affixed to the specimen; they appear in CT as small surface craters, providing
correspondences between the optical and CT coordinate systems.

For each pose the system records five frames: one \emph{dark} frame (ambient only)
and four \emph{single-LED} frames $B_s$, $s\in\{1,2,3,4\}$. Only one LED is lit per
frame, which is the requirement for a determined photometric-stereo system.

%=======================================================================
\section{Image Formation and Preprocessing}\label{sec:pre}
Raw frames are 8-bit sRGB. Four operations convert them to the linear,
single-channel luminances the solve requires.

\subsubsection{Ambient removal} the dark frame $B_0$ is subtracted,
$\tilde B_s=\max(B_s-B_0,\,0)$, removing ambient and sensor pedestal.

\subsubsection{Gamma linearisation} sRGB is decoded to linear radiance,
\begin{equation}
C_{\text{lin}}=\begin{cases}
C/12.92, & C\le0.04045\\
\big((C+0.055)/1.055\big)^{2.4}, & C>0.04045,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
applied per channel; photometric stereo is linear in radiance, so this step is
mandatory.

\subsubsection{Luminance} the linear channels are combined by Rec.\,601 weights,
$I_s=0.299R+0.587G+0.114B$.

\subsubsection{Specular inpainting} retroreflective markers and glints violate the
Lambertian assumption. Pixels that are simultaneously bright and low-saturation are
masked and filled by iterated Gaussian diffusion, so specular highlights do not
bias the normal solve.

\subsubsection{Exposure balancing} because the four single-LED frames are captured
separately, per-frame gain differs. Each frame is divided by its median,
$I_s\leftarrow I_s/\operatorname{median}(I_s)$, which removes a spurious global
brightness gradient between frames while preserving intra-frame spatial variation.

%=======================================================================
\section{Illumination Geometry and LED Index Mapping}\label{sec:led}
The solve requires the azimuth associated with each frame index. The physical
wiring is
\begin{equation}
1\!\to\!\ang{90}\,(\text{top}),\;2\!\to\!\ang{270},\;3\!\to\!\ang{180},\;
4\!\to\!\ang{0},
\end{equation}
with opposing pairs $(1,2)$ and $(3,4)$. This mapping is not cosmetic: an
incorrect assignment yields normals whose integrated depth is \emph{anti}-correlated
with CT ($r\approx-0.73$ on two validated levels), whereas the correct mapping
gives $r\approx+0.97/{+}0.99$. The mapping was resolved by exhaustively scoring all
$4!=24$ index$\to$azimuth permutations against CT marker depth and selecting the
maximiser; we recommend this check for any multi-LED rig.

%=======================================================================
\section{Photometric-Stereo Depth Estimation}\label{sec:ps}

\subsection{Far-Field Model}
Under a distant point source the image irradiance of a Lambertian surface is
\begin{equation}
I_s=\rho\,(\vn\!\cdot\!\vL_s),\qquad \vL_s=\text{const over the field.}
\label{eq:ff}
\end{equation}
Stacking the $S{=}4$ observations at a pixel, $\bm{I}=[I_1,\dots,I_S]^\top$ and
$\bm{L}=[\vL_1,\dots,\vL_S]^\top\in\mathbb{R}^{S\times3}$,
\begin{equation}
\bm{I}=\bm{L}\,\vg,\qquad \vg=\rho\vn,\qquad
\hat{\vg}=\bm{L}^{+}\bm{I},
\label{eq:ffsolve}
\end{equation}
where $\bm{L}^{+}$ is the Moore--Penrose pseudo-inverse ($\bm{L}^{-1}$ for $S{=}3$).
The albedo and normal are recovered as $\rho=\lVert\vg\rVert$ and
$\vn=\vg/\lVert\vg\rVert$, with the sign fixed so $n_z\ge0$ (camera-facing). The
solve is \emph{albedo-invariant}: $\rho$ appears only as the magnitude of $\vg$, so
surface orientation is independent of local reflectance.

\subsection{Normal Integration (Frankot--Chellappa)}\label{sec:fc}
Depth is obtained from the gradient field implied by the normals,
\begin{equation}
p=\frac{n_x}{n_z},\qquad q=-\frac{n_y}{n_z},
\label{eq:pq}
\end{equation}
(the sign on $q$ accounts for image rows increasing downward while $+y$ is up). The
least-squares-integrable depth is recovered in the frequency domain,
\begin{equation}
Z(u,v)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\!\left\{
\frac{-\,\jmath\omega_x\hat P-\jmath\omega_y\hat Q}
{\omega_x^2+\omega_y^2}\right\},
\label{eq:fc}
\end{equation}
with $\hat P,\hat Q$ the transforms of $p,q$ and the DC term set to zero. The
gradient field is even-reflected to $2H\times2W$ before the transform (a Neumann
boundary), preventing wrap-around ramps at the frame border. Equation~\eqref{eq:fc}
returns depth up to an additive constant---i.e.\ \emph{relative} depth; absolute
scale is fixed later by CT (Sec.~\ref{sec:affine}).

\subsection{Near-Field Point-Source Model}\label{sec:nf}
The far-field assumption $\vL_s=\text{const}$ fails for a rig whose lights sit
\SI{6}{\milli\meter} off-axis at \SI{55}{\milli\meter}: both the direction and the
intensity of each LED vary across the field, and integrating the mis-modelled
brightness gradient produces a spurious low-frequency curvature (the ``bowl'').

We work in a frame with $+x$ right, $+y$ up, $+z$ toward the camera. A pixel
$(u,v)$ at metric depth $D$ back-projects to
\begin{equation}
\vX(u,v,D)=D\Big[\tfrac{u-c_x}{f_x},\;-\tfrac{v-c_y}{f_y},\;-1\Big]^\top,
\label{eq:backproj}
\end{equation}
and LED $s$ sits at $\vP_s=[a_r\cos\phi_s,\,a_r\sin\phi_s,\,0]^\top$. The incident
direction and inverse-square attenuation at the surface point are
\begin{equation}
\vl_s=\frac{\vP_s-\vX}{\lVert\vP_s-\vX\rVert},\qquad
w_s=\frac{1}{\lVert\vP_s-\vX\rVert^{2}}.
\label{eq:nflight}
\end{equation}
The point-source image model $I_s=\rho\,(\vn\!\cdot\!\vl_s)\,w_s$ is linearised by
pre-multiplying out the attenuation,
\begin{equation}
m_s\;:=\;I_s\,\lVert\vP_s-\vX\rVert^{2}\;=\;\rho\,(\vn\!\cdot\!\vl_s)
=\vg\!\cdot\!\vl_s ,
\label{eq:nflin}
\end{equation}
which is again \emph{linear} in $\vg=\rho\vn$ but with a \emph{per-pixel} design
matrix $\bm{\Lambda}(u,v)=[\vl_1,\dots,\vl_S]^\top$. The per-pixel solve is
\begin{equation}
\hat{\vg}(u,v)=\big(\bm{\Lambda}^\top\bm{\Lambda}+\epsilon\bm I\big)^{-1}
\bm{\Lambda}^\top\bm{m},
\label{eq:nfsolve}
\end{equation}
with $\bm{m}=[m_1,\dots,m_S]^\top$ and a small Tikhonov term $\epsilon$. Because
$\bm{\Lambda}$ and $w_s$ depend on the unknown depth $D$, the solve is iterated
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:nf}): $D$ is bootstrapped as a plane at the CT working distance,
normals are solved and integrated~\eqref{eq:fc}, the relative depth is affine-mapped
to metric depth on the fiducials~\eqref{eq:affine}, and the geometry
\eqref{eq:backproj}--\eqref{eq:nflight} is recomputed. Four iterations suffice. LED
positions $\vP_s$ are \emph{assumed} from the ring geometry---the principal
approximation of this work.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Near-field photometric-stereo depth}\label{alg:nf}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State $D\gets$ plane at $\bar D_{\text{CT}}$ (mean fiducial depth)
\For{$k=1$ to $4$}
  \State compute $\vX,\vl_s,w_s$ from $D$ via \eqref{eq:backproj}--\eqref{eq:nflight}
  \State $m_s\gets I_s\lVert\vP_s-\vX\rVert^2$; solve $\hat\vg$ per pixel \eqref{eq:nfsolve}
  \State $\vn\gets\hat\vg/\lVert\hat\vg\rVert$, orient $n_z\ge0$
  \State $Z\gets$ integrate$(\vn)$ \eqref{eq:pq}--\eqref{eq:fc}
  \State $(a,b)\gets$ fit $z_{\text{lens}}=aZ+b$ on fiducials \eqref{eq:affine}
  \State $D\gets aZ+b$
\EndFor
\State \Return $D$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Inverse-Square Baseline}\label{sec:invsq}
For comparison we also estimate range directly from brightness. Combining the four
attenuation-limited frames, $I\propto\rho\,\Phi/r^2$, gives
\begin{equation}
r=\frac{\sqrt{\rho\,\Phi}}{\sqrt{I}}\ \Longrightarrow\ r\propto I^{-1/2}.
\label{eq:invsq}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:invsq} recovers range only if albedo $\rho$ is constant. On
textured bone it is not, so dark, low-albedo pixels are read as ``far.'' This
baseline is included precisely to quantify the failure it produces
(Sec.~\ref{sec:results}), motivating the normal-based solve.

%=======================================================================
\section{Camera Calibration and Pose}\label{sec:calib}
Intrinsics $\K$ and distortion are estimated from a ChArUco target. The working
focal length is $f_x\approx\SI{860}{px}$; it is validated by four-point PnP
reprojection with a residual of \SI{0.42}{px}, i.e.
\begin{equation}
\tfrac1N\!\sum_i\big\lVert\pi(\K[\R|\tvec]\vX_i)-\bm u_i\big\rVert=\SI{0.42}{px}.
\end{equation}
Detection and the photometric solve are performed on \emph{raw} (non-undistorted)
frames, because the endoscope's strong radial distortion ($k_3$ large) otherwise
drops peripheral markers; distortion is handled analytically inside the projection
$\pi(\cdot)$ and the back-projection.

%=======================================================================
\section{CT Registration}\label{sec:reg}
Registration converts the relative photometric surface into absolute CT coordinates
and provides the ground truth for evaluation.

\subsection{Dual-modality correspondence}
Each retroreflective fiducial gives a pixel $\bm u_i$ (sparkle/dark-ring detection,
aggregated across the four frames with a $\ge3$-frame quorum to reject false
positives) and a CT coordinate $\vX_i^{\text{CT}}$ (the marker crater in CT). Markers
belonging to adjacent vertebral levels are excluded, so each solve uses one rigid
body only.

\subsection{Perspective-$n$-Point pose}
The CT$\to$camera pose minimises reprojection over the $N\ge4$ non-coplanar
fiducials of one vertebra,
\begin{equation}
(\R,\tvec)=\arg\min_{\R,\tvec}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
\big\lVert\pi\!\big(\K[\R|\tvec]\vX_i^{\text{CT}}\big)-\bm u_i\big\rVert^2,
\label{eq:pnp}
\end{equation}
solved by SQPnP~\cite{sqpnp}, which is globally optimal and, unlike the iterative
DLT, is stable at $N{=}4$. The metric depth of each fiducial follows as
\begin{equation}
z^{\text{CT}}_i=\big[\R\vX_i^{\text{CT}}+\tvec\big]_z .
\label{eq:ctdepth}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Affine depth calibration}\label{sec:affine}
The photometric depth $Z$ is relative; the fiducials fix its scale and offset by
least squares,
\begin{equation}
(a,b)=\arg\min_{a,b}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\big(a\,Z(\bm u_i)+b-z^{\text{CT}}_i\big)^2,
\qquad D=aZ+b.
\label{eq:affine}
\end{equation}
Crucially, $(a,b)$ are only two scalars: they set global scale and offset and
\emph{cannot} deform the surface. All shape---and hence any residual bowl---is fixed
by the photometric solve (Secs.~\ref{sec:ps}), a point verified experimentally in
Sec.~\ref{sec:results}.

\subsection{Back-projection into CT coordinates}
Each kept pixel is lifted to camera coordinates using the calibrated intrinsics
(distortion inverted by \texttt{undistortPoints}), and transformed to CT space by
the inverse pose,
\begin{equation}
\vX^{\text{cam}}=D\,\K^{-1}[\,\bm u\,;1],\qquad
\vX^{\text{CT}}=\R^{\top}\big(\vX^{\text{cam}}-\tvec\big).
\label{eq:tocT}
\end{equation}
The result is a coloured point set in the same millimetre CT frame as the model.

\subsection{Per-vertebra rationale}
Because the spine flexes at the intervertebral discs, no single rigid transform
fits multiple levels; each vertebra is therefore registered independently. A single
rigid multi-level fit is computed only as a rigidity check on a static specimen and
is not used for accuracy reporting.

%=======================================================================
\section{Surface Extraction}\label{sec:surf}
\subsection{Confidence masking}
A pixel is retained if it is (i)~bright enough (bone-mask threshold on albedo),
(ii)~photometrically consistent---the per-pixel residual
\begin{equation}
\varrho(u,v)=\frac{\sum_s\big|I_s-(\hat\vg\!\cdot\!\vl_s)w_s\big|}{\sum_s I_s}
<0.22,
\label{eq:resid}
\end{equation}
which rejects specular marker cores and shadowed pixels, (iii)~within a metric band
about the fiducial depths, and (iv)~not a gross depth spike (median-filter test).
The frame border is cropped (FFT wrap-around), the largest connected components are
kept, and a morphological opening removes speckle and thin integration ``fans.''

\subsection{Mask-aware smoothing and meshing}
Depth used for the mesh is smoothed with a normalised-convolution Gaussian that does
not bleed across mask holes,
\begin{equation}
D_{\text{sm}}=\frac{G_\sigma\!*(wD)}{G_\sigma\!*w},\qquad w=\mathbb{1}[\text{kept}].
\end{equation}
Neighbouring kept pixels are triangulated on the image grid; a quad is emitted only
if all four corners are kept and their depth span is $<\SI{3}{\milli\meter}$, so the
mesh does not web across discontinuities. Per-vertebra meshes are written in CT
coordinates and optionally concatenated into a combined multi-level surface
(\num{432757} vertices across five levels).

%=======================================================================
\section{Evaluation Metrics}\label{sec:metrics}
\subsubsection{3-D fiducial registration error} the primary metric is the Euclidean
distance in CT coordinates between each reconstructed fiducial and its ground truth,
\begin{equation}
\text{FRE}=\sqrt{\tfrac1N\textstyle\sum_i\lVert
\hat\vX_i^{\text{CT}}-\vX_i^{\text{CT,gt}}\rVert^2},
\label{eq:fre}
\end{equation}
where $\hat\vX_i^{\text{CT}}$ is obtained by \eqref{eq:tocT} at the fiducial pixel.

\subsubsection{Plane-fit deviation} a proxy for residual low-frequency shape error:
the RMS distance of the surface to its best-fit plane, computed from the smallest
singular value of the centred vertex matrix.

\subsubsection{Working distance and coplanarity} the working distance is the mean
fiducial depth $\bar z^{\text{CT}}$ \eqref{eq:ctdepth}; marker coplanarity is the
RMS out-of-plane thickness of the fiducials (smallest singular value of the centred
CT coordinates), reported to characterise pose conditioning.

\subsection{Far-field vs.\ near-field FRE}
Table~\ref{tab:fre} reports the 3-D FRE~\eqref{eq:fre}. On the three
well-conditioned 4-marker levels (4--6) the near-field model reaches

\subsection{Inverse-square baseline}
With identical CT anchoring, the inverse-square estimator~\eqref{eq:invsq} produced
\SIrange{7.8}{9.6}{\milli\meter} FRE, dense relief inflating to
\SIrange{150}{559}{\milli\meter} (true CT range \SIrange{17}{26}{\milli\meter}), and
a fitted depth slope of the wrong sign---all direct consequences of the constant-
albedo assumption. This quantifies why an albedo-invariant normal solve is required.

\subsection{Failure modes (reported in full)}
Two levels did not reach millimetre accuracy, for reasons unrelated to working
distance (all levels are at \SIrange{51}{60}{\milli\meter}). \textbf{Level 7} has
only three fiducials, so the PnP pose~\eqref{eq:pnp} is under-constrained (P3P admits
up to four solutions). \textbf{Level 8} has the \emph{best} marker geometry
(Table~\ref{tab:geom}) yet the near-field iteration (Algorithm~\ref{alg:nf})
diverged, collapsing the confidence mask~\eqref{eq:resid}; the far-field solve on the
same data remained stable at \SI{5.66}{\milli\meter}. The cause is a solver
robustness issue, not a geometric limit, and is unresolved; we report it rather than
omit it.

\section{Implementation and Reproducibility}
The pipeline is implemented in Python (NumPy, OpenCV, SciPy, Matplotlib). Depth solves
run per pixel over the full frame via batched linear algebra; the near-field solve
uses batched $3\times3$ normal equations~\eqref{eq:nfsolve}. Registration uses OpenCV
\texttt{solvePnP} with the \texttt{SQPNP} flag and \texttt{undistortPoints} for
back-projection. Meshes and fiducials are exported to the Slicer \texttt{.mrk.json}
and PLY formats; the RAS/LPS convention of exported markers is matched to the model
so overlays register on load. Module map: photometric core and integration
(\texttt{bone\_depth\_batch}), near-field solve and registration
(\texttt{nearfield\_ct}, \texttt{register\_*\_ct}), inverse-square baseline
(\texttt{inverse\_square\_*}), fiducial and mesh export (\texttt{export\_fiducials},
\texttt{combine\_all}).

%=======================================================================
\section{Discussion and Limitations}
The dominant residual error is the use of \emph{assumed} LED positions $\vP_s$: a
mirror-sphere or matte-target calibration~\cite{queau} of $\vP_s$ is expected to
further reduce the near-field residual. The near-coaxial geometry
($\alpha\approx\ang{6}$) intrinsically compresses recovered relief because the
tilt-encoding signal scales with $\sin\alpha$. Validation here is on one instrumented
specimen; repeatability, additional specimens, and an independent
surface-to-surface metric are the natural extensions. Finally, the level-8 near-field
divergence should be diagnosed and the solver hardened.

%=======================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
We have documented the complete LuminaBone pipeline: linear image formation,
far-field and near-field photometric-stereo solves with explicit per-pixel
equations, Frankot--Chellappa integration, an inverse-square baseline, PnP-based CT
registration with affine depth calibration, confidence-masked meshing, and the
evaluation metrics. On three well-conditioned lumbar levels the near-field model
registers to \SIrange{1.3}{1.7}{\milli\meter} 3-D FRE and removes the far-field
bowl; the inverse-square baseline and two failure modes are reported without
omission. Every stage is specified at the equation level for independent
reproduction.
